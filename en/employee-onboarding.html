HR & organization

Good onboarding starts before the first day

Help a new starter know who to meet, what to do and where to ask for help.

Practical guide • Updated 11 September 2026TH / EN
In this guide1. Send useful instructions before arrival2. Give the first day a clear path3. Check understanding, not just completionAction checklistSources
THE STARTING POINT

CIPD describes induction as helping newcomers understand their organization, role and working environment. Plan beyond first-day paperwork.

CIPD ↗

Practical application to your own work

1. Send useful instructions before arrival

Send a concise note covering time, location, contact person and preparation. Check that equipment and role-appropriate access have owners. Avoid requesting information that is not yet needed.

2. Give the first day a clear path

Name the people who welcome the starter, introduce the team and explain the first task. Offer a realistic starting task and a clear way to ask questions.

3. Check understanding, not just completion

Ask whether expectations are clear, which systems remain inaccessible and where help is needed. Record actions and owners so onboarding connects with everyday work.

Before you put it into practice

  • ✓The starter knows when, where and whom to meet
  • ✓Equipment and access have owners
  • ✓Follow-up is scheduled
